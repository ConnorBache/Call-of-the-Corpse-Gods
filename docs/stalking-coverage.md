# Native Tiger and Sabretooth stalking

`monster_stalking::turn` ports frozen `enemyStalkingTurn`: disposition guards, four-cell hunt threshold, observed-only movement direction and last-seen position, three doorway waits, peek action and following-tick bypass, unseen retreat-goal probes, hazard/distance/cell-index ranking and exact hazard-aware movement. It runs after general monster AI in the normal scheduler; source haste actions do not invoke this helper, but do pursue the existing stored stalking goal using movement-only path steps.

Twenty raw source fixtures compare complete state, RNG and presentation events, including stationary/moving observations, doorway boundaries, hidden/hazard/blocked goals, rooted actors and both authored species. Two additional complete-state/event cases verify clean and dirty offscreen catch-up: source stalking actors do not pursue their stored goal offscreen. Six mixed command traces pass doorway waits/peek, moving quarry, close hunting, lost quarry, Sabretooth fast actions and player haste. Every current state field, RNG, presentation event, reconstructed history opening and pending haste snapshot is compared strictly. These cases do not imply all bestiary traits or interactions are supported.

## Complexity

For A actors, V floor cells, G grates, B bulwarks and Q visibility-query cost, each action checks eight doorway neighbors and at most25 nearby cells. Candidate eligibility scans actors and terrain structures; ranking is a single stable scan, O(25*(A+B+Q+hazard lookup)) plus actor-state copy cost. At most one creature path query adds O(V*(G+B+hazard lookup)+V log V) worst-case time and O(V) scratch under current native pathfield implementation. A move additionally incurs engine collision and movement hooks. Actor-vector COW mutation can copy its full floor block. No sorting or gameplay random draw is added by the stalking helper; path/move dependencies retain their source RNG. Bounds are analytical, not benchmark claims.
