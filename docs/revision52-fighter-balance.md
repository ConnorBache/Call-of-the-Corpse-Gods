# Revision 52 natural Fighter balance

## Designer instruction and target

Assume all generated natural hearts are collected. Treat an always-spawned pack as one Fighter and fight its members sequentially without restoring player HP. Projected lifetime damage rises by two **percentage points** at floors 5, 10, and so on: the provisional 20% anchor gives 20% on floors 1–4 and 36% at floor 40. The 20% anchor is provisional because the designer gave it as an example. Allowed levers are HP/Dodge/DR, Accuracy/damage, and pack count. This pass uses the first two and leaves pack counts and rewards unchanged.

The metric is the population-weighted median across each species' native mean gross lifetime damage as a fraction of the matched floor-entry player maximum HP. It is not the median individual random fight. Ursa Minor then Major constitute one case. Damage includes residual venom settlement, before healing; player regeneration is tracked separately. The player begins each independent case at full health. This does not claim an entire floor/campaign is survivable without healing.

## Scope and distinct identities

The 20 explicit Fighters are Blorb, Rat, Shellback Beetle, Hemogoblin, Pale Jackal, Needleling, Pale Biter, Giant Beetle, Glim, Bear, Long Saidri, Pale Greater Biter, Troll, Dire Bear, Killspawn, Phoenix, Cancer, Ursa Minor, Ursa Major, and Minotaur. Blorb intentionally remains negligible trash. Hybrids and other roles are not certified by this Fighter pass. Existing abilities, traits, speed, regeneration, attack pattern and status effects remain active in native simulation. Distinct desired durability bands guide fitting before damage is fitted, rather than assigning every Fighter identical HP or damage.

A fixed 150-row catalog stores five integral stats for observed species/floor births. HP is always the native canonical 100 + 6 × Defense. All effective stats and HP are at least their R51 baseline values. No random draws are added. Population recipes, IDs, heart definitions, level-up grants and spawn danger budgets are unchanged. Natural appearances outside an authored species' depth window use its nearest authored floor, with lower floor winning ties. Unknown and contextual summon keys are a no-op.

## Ownership and compatibility

Apply only at new floor-aware natural birth boundaries, after inherited R51 overlays. Generic constructors, summoning overlays, load/save, copying, time travel, off-screen travel, wounds, growth and resurrection never reapply profiles. Fungus-feast bodies receive their profile before being marked dead and zeroed. No model fields or save schema are added. Exact revision 52 is required; older and fractional/future revisions are untouched.

Native enemy health synchronization and revival derive capacity from Defense. Arbitrary HP alone would be lost, so catalog Defense is integral and compatible with both paths. Increasing HP also increases any existing HP-dependent effect such as corpse explosions. Longer fights can permit conditional summons or feeding during play; the all-heart input census excludes such additional dynamic rewards and does not model infinite farming.

## Reproducible model and validation

The baseline uses 256 native generated worlds × 40 floors and native heart consumption with the Unblessed level-up grants. Player stat vectors remain joint rather than independently pairing medians. It includes initially generated living natural hearts, including initial killable Zillennia, and ordinary carried/ground hearts. It excludes dream bodies, nightmares, heartless actors, initial corpses, conditional summons/fish and repeated farming. Optional equipment and chosen abilities are absent. Floor entry means hearts from earlier floors, not pre-collecting the current floor. The player physical base is R51/R52 31.5 + 2 × Offense.

The fitter uses 192 worlds and 256 native fights per candidate case. Profile selection is stratified over the complete occurrence-weighted joint distribution, not a prefix. Another 64 worlds are held out. Each cohort is validated with 512 fresh combat seeds per case: 145 cases × 512 trials × two cohorts × before/after = 296,960 validation fights, of which 148,480 use R52 dispatch. The 192-world cohort has maximum floor-median error 1.71 percentage points. The held-out 64-world cohort has a worst deviation of -5.19 points at floor 7 (16.81% against 22% target) and a maximum positive deviation of +3.77 points. Its floor40 result is 36.39%. This finite-sample variation is disclosed: exponential Accuracy/Dodge interaction and different heart distributions mean a fixed floor catalog cannot guarantee an identical curve in every run.

The combined, occurrence-weighted estimate over all 256 worlds is within 1.72 points of target at every floor. Selected before → after percentages: floor 1 7.11 → 18.28; 5 0.00 → 22.28; 10 2.81 → 23.51; 20 6.60 → 27.91; 30 7.21 → 32.02; 40 4.54 → 36.28. There are 81 player deaths (0.055%) in the 148,480 after-fights and no horizon censors. Deaths truncate theoretical full enemy lifetimes and are not counted as successful kills. The largest sampled single packet is 32.08% of player maximum HP; this is not a guaranteed universal cap.

World seeds are depth-balance-r51-000000 through depth-balance-r51-000255. Indices 0–191 are the calibration cohort and 192–255 the independent world holdout. Raw world and trial evidence, prepared requests and fitting scripts are retained in the floor-depth-balance/rebalance52 cloud audit. The delivered audit contains all 40 floor rows, all species/floor rows, separate calibration/holdout tables, the catalog and reproducibility scripts. No optional abilities/equipment or complete campaign survival claim is made. The approved Blorb and Killspawn follow-ups are now included in a fresh 148,480-fight R52 recheck. Blorb adds one exact-scale caustic packet after a positive melee hit. Killspawn adds rounded half of actual melee damage as additive installments over the next three normal turns, with complete post-death settlement included. Killspawn Offense alone was refitted on floors 25–34; every HP/Defense/Dodge/DR/Accuracy and pack count remains unchanged. Its floor-30 direct-plus-bleed result is now 32.02%, rather than the 49.38% observed before compensating its direct damage. Dragon flight is a separate Fighter-hybrid mechanic and is not certified by this pure-Fighter combat curve.

Focused integration evidence: three catalog/unit tests, four birth/health/save/revival/copy tests, six revision/save/replay/generation tests, plus a separate public-dispatch Blorb check: ten positive native hits caused exactly ten extra caustic damage (57 versus 67 gross damage). Legacy parity and untouched-Fighter checks are recorded in the follow-up receipt. The original 1,160 paired R51/R52 outcomes applied before the intentional contact mechanics were added and are not claimed as current R52 equivalence. The generation test verifies two complete 40-floor seeds: only explicitly asserted natural birth profile deltas differ; population, IDs, rewards and the remaining serialized world remain identical. These focused results are not a complete release/workspace test claim.

## Complexity and portability

Let R be catalog rows (fixed 150), B its embedded JSON bytes (about 21 KB), and N new natural actors. The first admitted lookup parses the catalog once through OnceLock in O(B) time and O(B) retained allocation. Every new birth scans R rows to find the nearest exact-key depth: O(R) time, O(1) extra scratch space, then five stats and HP are assigned in O(1). A generated floor adds O(NR) work plus the one-time parse; no pathfinding, RNG, combat, or render loops are hidden in this lookup. The surrounding existing actor allocation, trait construction, floor generation and copy-on-write work remain additive and are not claimed constant. Every rejected legacy revision returns before catalog parsing. The catalog has a bounded compile-time size; timing is unmeasured and should be profiled separately if generation cost is a concern. Native tests cover finite/integral unique rows, exact revision gating, preserved rewards and traits, and health/save persistence.
