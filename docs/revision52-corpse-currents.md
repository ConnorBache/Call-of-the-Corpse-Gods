# Revision 52 corpse currents and fallen flyers

## Authored rule and scope

The designer requested: “Corpses (even those of flying enemies), items, etc.
should be affected by currents. Flying corpses aren't flying and fall.”

R52 loose waking corpses now move one cell with a legal current during each
normal environmental update. Dead actors never qualify for run-aware flight,
whether their living flight came from innate traits, a timer, a Vampire form,
Ascend, equipment or Phase Through. Flight-granting traits remain stored for
revival; this is a physical eligibility change, not deletion of their identity.
Living flyers retain current immunity and their existing movement/fall rules.

Ordinary ground items already followed currents. That implementation remains
in place and runs once, after corpse drift. Hearts, equipment and consumables
remain in the ground-item layer. Existing Drown pickup-radius protection,
Phantasmii Shard anchoring and dream-owned item exclusions are retained. These
are established exceptions, not newly introduced ones. Carried gear is not
independently moved by this corpse change; existing carried-item floating rules
still apply. Walls, doors, lights and other structural fixtures are not loose
objects. No new random corpse bobbing or item behavior is introduced.

## Timing, occupancy and roots

Movement uses the saved arrow and the existing revision-aware legal destination
predicate: ordinary water or an admitted safe bank. The change does not rewrite
current directions, lake generation, Floods, shoreline selection or terrain.
Arrows into walls, blocked water, stairs, pits or chasms remain inadmissible for
ordinary current displacement. This request does not create waterfalls or
extend the current graph across floors.

Corpses have their own existing occupancy layer, separate from living actors
and items. A living creature and an item do not stop a corpse sharing their
cell. Snapshot corpse occupancy forbids swaps and following a body that vacates
its cell during the same pass. Stable numeric actor IDs resolve competing
claims; a destination is reserved only after movement. Existing alternate-bank
selection handles an occupied shoreline endpoint without stacking corpses.
There is no fallback placement search, ID allocation or deletion in drift.

Fully connected, living fungal roots continue to anchor their corpses. An
incomplete conduit does not yet anchor its target. When that corpse drifts,
existing root severing retains the pair's inactive history and clears orphaned
root material. Revival age, health, abilities, inventory and death state are
unchanged by displacement. Reverse Entropy's existing corpse-age freeze is
not converted into physical anchoring.

Corpse drift runs in waking environmental cleanup, including while the player
Dreamwalks. The separate dream environmental pass does not move waking bodies
again. It is not an extra player Haste/Fast action. Offscreen corpse-only pools
with usable currents or unsupported bodies over gaps now require precise normal
turns instead of stationary catch-up. Occupied-but-legal destinations remain
conservative because a later revival can release a body within the interval.
Absent/zero currents and statically illegal arrows remain bulk-eligible; fully
rooted bodies do not activate the new current guard.

## Chasm falls and persistence

Actual death already calls the shared corpse-placement routine immediately.
That routine ignores the dead creature's flight, follows same-coordinate chasms
through successive floors, resolves corpse occupancy independently of items
and living actors, and retains the stable actor ID. Death drops use the existing
ground-item placement routine. Floor-collapse also already applies these two
placement paths to corpses and items while leaving a living flyer aloft.
Those algorithms and their existing bottomless/full-floor disposal behavior
are not replaced. Only physically required missing floors are generated by
these placement callbacks; ordinary drift generates no floors.

A second R52 check settles a waking corpse already over a chasm or pit, including
an old saved body or a gap opened by Black Pit. It runs at command admission,
before the normal phase's clocks/revival, at command completion, before inactive
corpse upkeep, and after environmental temporary-terrain expiry but before chasm
restoration. Black Pit therefore drops the body by completion of even a free
Haste/Fast action, without consuming another world turn. A mature unsupported
corpse falls before it can revive at its old position. Ordinary root links do
not suspend bodies over missing ground; existing fall placement severs them.
A gap existing only in the dream plane does not transfer a waking corpse.
Save/load itself does not mutate this spatial state; the next command or precise
inactive update settles it. Read-only observation does not run physics.

The new behavior uses existing actor coordinates, current arrays and root
records. There is no save schema, timer, migration, new random draw or separate
physical copy. Storage, Snapshot and See the Future carry and restore the same
state. R52 loaded runs use the corrected current/flight behavior; R51 and older
corpse drift/flight predicates retain their historical rules. Existing immediate
corpse falls already apply across revisions. R52 replay outcomes can therefore
differ from recordings made before this correction.

## Complexity

Let F be loaded floors (at most 40), A actors on the affected floor, C its dead
actors, K their per-actor payload, R the relevant fungal-root records, L their
path length, V floor cells, d the bounded stable-ID trie depth (at most 16), and
B the current destination predicate cost. B includes grate scans, door-key
lookup and bank feature/portal/solid-object eligibility; it is not universally
constant time.

Each normal drift pass collects and sorts C IDs/cells in O(A + C log C) time
and O(C) scratch. Destination/root checks add O(C*(B + R*(A+L) + F + d)) time.
The first trusted actor lookup can reconcile a cold identity index at its
existing O(total persisted identities*d + F + V) cost. Corpse writes can detach
an actor block at O(A*K), and the first dirty floor write can detach O(V + floor
metadata). These copy-on-write costs are not paid as deep copies per corpse.

Each actual move reuses root severing: scan the loaded floors' root records,
mark matching records inactive and clear orphaned material only on affected
floors. Its additional worst-case time is O(sum(R_f) + sum_changed(V_f +
R_f*L_f)); scratch is O(max_changed(V_f + R_f*L_f)) under the existing root
helper, plus affected floor copy-on-write costs. No pathfinding, rendering,
native rasterization or gameplay RNG is added.

Gravity settlement also scans A actors at each stated boundary. For G bodies
actually over gaps, it clones/sorts them in O(A + G*K + G log G) time with
O(G*K) scratch, then pays established placement, root-sever and any required
floor-generation costs. G is zero in an already-settled ordinary scene. There
is no extra time advancement; multiple scans at a command boundary are
idempotent, not multiple drift steps.

The offscreen activity guard costs O(A*(B + R*(A+L))) in the worst case with
O(1) scratch, after existing floor/plane reads. If active, elapsed normal turns
retain the existing exact environment/actor simulation cost. There is no new
elapsed-time cap or stationary approximation. A blocked circulating pool can
therefore keep an inactive floor dynamic; this is conservative correctness,
not a measured optimization claim.

Dead-flight eligibility adds O(1) to each run-aware flight query and can skip
more expensive trait/equipment lookup for dead actors. Existing placement/fall
costs remain O(sum(V_f + occupancy_f)) across at most 40 floors plus native
generation callbacks and root severing. Tests and future profiling should use
large corpse pools, crowded banks, many root links and long inactive intervals.
These are analytical bounds, not timing measurements.

## Verification

Focused regression sources are `revision52_corpse_currents_tests.rs` (core)
and `revision52_corpse_fall_observation_tests.rs` (desktop observation). They
cover direct drift, flight sources/revival, independent occupancy, stable-ID
collisions, roots, normal/Haste timing, exact inactive catch-up, storage,
Snapshot/Future restoration, multilevel death drops, floor collapse, historical
revisions, existing saved gaps, mature corpses, pre-restoration gravity, Black
Pit in normal/Haste phases, and a fresh post-command visible-source/lower-floor
observation.

Execution receipts and their pass/fail status are recorded under
`verification/revision52-corpse-currents/`. Focused native results are not a
full-workspace, Windows, interactive or release-verification claim. The release
owner retains the combined final gate.
